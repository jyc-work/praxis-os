# Methodology

> **How a PraxisOS user actually operates.** The system is a discipline, not a dashboard.

## Cadence

| Review | Frequency | Time budget | Core question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly | every week | 10–20 min | What changed? Did I violate a principle? |
| Monthly | every month | 30–60 min | Ability, assets, optionality, dependency, energy, attention |
| Quarterly | every quarter | 60–90 min | Am I more free, or more dependent? |
| Annual | every year | half a day | Trajectory, constitution, direction |
| Decision review | on schedule (30d/90d/365d) | 20 min | Was my judgment good, apart from the outcome? |

## Weekly review — the five questions

Weekly review answers exactly five questions and stops:

1. What was the most important event this week?
2. What important judgments did I make?
3. Did I violate any of my principles?
4. Did I discover a new principle candidate?
5. What is the *one* thing to change next week?

If it takes more than 20 minutes, you are over-reviewing.

## Monthly review — the six capitals

```text
Ability       — what can I do now that I could not do before?
Assets        — what accumulates regardless of daily mood?
Optionality   — how many doors stay open?
Dependency    — how much of my life depends on one person/org/source?
Energy        — am I running on reserves?
Attention     — where does my attention actually go?
```

One trend line, not ten metrics.

## Quarterly review — the freedom audit

The master question:

> **过去三个月，我是在变得更加自由，还是更加依赖？**

Then walk the domains: career, money, skills, relationships, health, time,
projects, learning. Then the hardest comparison:

```text
my behaviour  vs  my stated values
```

Where they diverge, that is either a value you do not really hold, or a
behaviour you are not really choosing.

## Decision review — judgment vs. outcome

For every significant decision, when its review date arrives:

```text
What did I predict?
What actually happened?
Where was I wrong?
What information did I ignore?
Was the decision wrong — or was the outcome merely unlucky?
Which principle needs updating?
```

**Decision Quality ≠ Outcome Quality.** A poor outcome does not make a good
decision bad, and a good outcome does not validate a sloppy process. Frozen
predictions (written at decision time) are what make this audit honest.

## Experiment methodology

When information is missing, run a small experiment instead of more analysis:

- **Small** — bounded cost in time, money and attention.
- **Reversible** — can be stopped at any point without lasting damage.
- **Measurable** — success is defined by a metric before starting.
- **Time-boxed** — start and end dates in the file.

Turn "the job market is bad, I think" into "submit 20 applications, measure
resume-pass, first-round, second-round and offer rates".

## Principle lifecycle discipline

```text
candidate → testing → validated → core
                ↘         ↘        ↘
                 revised ←─────────┘ → retired
```

- `candidate`: an idea worth testing, not yet trusted.
- `testing`: actively seeking confirming AND disconfirming evidence.
- `validated`: survived repeated real-world checks. **Only the human promotes.**
- `core`: load-bearing; still falsifiable.
- `revised`: the wording or scope changed; old version stays in Revision History.
- `retired`: disconfirmed or obsolete. It stays visible in the file history.

Rule of thumb: if a principle has no `Boundary` or `Counter Evidence`, it is a
slogan — keep it a candidate.

## The overthinking guard

PraxisOS is itself a tool that can be over-built. When you catch yourself
reorganising the system instead of living:

> **PraxisOS 服务现实，不是现实服务 PraxisOS。**
> PraxisOS serves reality; reality does not serve PraxisOS.

When in doubt, prefer the 24-hour controlled action over the 90-minute analysis.
